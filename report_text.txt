Executive summary
The objective is to refine the hourly battery-sizing procedure described in the uploaded thesis and the
follow-up formula flowchart. The thesis develops Australian residential load profiles and an hourly PVgeneration method but primarily reports the no-battery energy mismatch. Its Section 6.17 reports annual
solar generation of 10,539 kWh, household demand of 5,780 kWh, direct solar use of 2,668 kWh, PV
surplus of 7,871 kWh and grid imports of 3,112 kWh [1]. These aggregate results are internally energyconsistent, but do not uniquely determine an optimal battery capacity.
The corrected formulation uses separate electrical energy and state-of-charge variables, usable-energy
limits, maximum charge/discharge power, energy conservation at the AC bus, a zero-kWh baseline, cyclicstate handling, explicit tariffs and a discounted ownership cost. The principal objective selects the
candidate capacity minimising annual equivalent total cost or, equivalently under the same assumptions,
maximising net benefit against the PV-only baseline. A policy-aware subsidy function replaces an
automatic rebate, and tariff-aware dispatch is distinguished from the baseline greedy strategy [2], [4]–[9].
A controlled, reproducible 8,760-hour illustrative reconstruction is provided only to check model
behaviour and demonstrate the graphs. It is calibrated to the FIVE annual aggregate values in the
uploaded thesis; this calibration does not recreate the unavailable measured/synthetic time sequence. The
illustrative economic assumptions, not thesis-supplied prices, produce a minimum at 0 kWh, a valid result
illustrating the possibility that battery ownership is uneconomic under a particular scenario.

4. Complete proposed mathematical function
4.1 PV generation and temporal mismatch
E_PV,t = P_PV × g_t ;

g_t = (G_POA,t / G_STC) × PR_t × Δt (1)

The thesis retains 6.6 kW and PR = 0.80 for its baseline. G_POA refers to plane-of-array irradiance,
whereas using the available global horizontal irradiance (GHI) without transposition is a documented
approximation. Actual PV output should also respect AC inverter export/generation limits when
independently known [1], [10].
S_t = max(0, E_PV,t − E_L,t) ;

D_t = max(0, E_L,t − E_PV,t) (2)

For each hour, PV directly supplies the contemporaneous load before the battery acts. Thus only S_t can
charge storage under PV-only charging, and only D_t can be replaced by battery discharge. Positive S_t
and D_t cannot occur simultaneously.

4.2 Corrected battery limits and state transition
E_c,t = min{ S_t, P_c,max Δt, (α_max E_b − B_(t−1)) / η_c } (3)
E_d,t = min{ D_t, P_d,max Δt, η_d [B_(t−1) − α_min E_b] } (4)
B_t = B_(t−1) + η_c E_c,t − E_d,t / η_d (5)
α_min E_b ≤ B_t ≤ α_max E_b ; SOC_t(%) = 100 B_t / E_b (E_b > 0) (6)
For the illustrated numerical experiments only, α_min = 0.10, α_max = 0.95, η_c = η_d = 0.95 and a 0.5 Crate are assumed, i.e., P_c,max = P_d,max = 0.5E_b kW. These are transparent study assumptions, not
claims that all marketed batteries have identical power ratings. Hardware-specific AC power ratings,
inverter constraints and efficiencies should replace them when known [9].

At E_b = 0, define E_c,t = E_d,t = B_t = 0 without calculating SOC. For E_b > 0, simulate the 8,760-hour series
to a repeatable cyclic state such that B_8760 ≈ B_0, or impose a suitable terminal energy constraint. This

4.3 Grid exchange and energy conservation
E_i,t = D_t − E_d,t ; E_x,t = S_t − E_c,t − E_curt,t (7)
E_PV,t + E_i,t + E_d,t = E_L,t + E_c,t + E_x,t + E_curt,t (8)
In the nonnegative-FiT baseline, curtailment E_curt,t is fixed at zero. If exporting is disallowed, capped or
charged a negative price, a curtailment control variable may be enabled, with 0 ≤ E_curt,t ≤ S_t − E_c,t
and separate export-cap constraints. Equation (8) must equal zero residual in every simulated hour.

4.4 Annual grid cost and ownership cost
C_grid(E_b) = Σ(t=1…8760)[ T_i,t E_i,t − T_FIT,t E_x,t ] (9)
C_net(E_b) = C_0(E_b) − S_gov(E_b) (10)
CRF(r,L) = r(1+r)^L / [(1+r)^L − 1] ;

CRF(0,L) = 1/L (11)

AEC(E_b) = CRF(r,L) C_net(E_b) + C_O&M(E_b) (12)
AEC is an equivalent annual ownership cost. The gross installed cost must include the whole installed
battery and any incremental inverter/electrical work, not just cell cost. Fixed household supply charges
cancel in battery-versus-no-battery comparisons only when tariff schedules do not change with capacity.
Full NPV with replacements, salvage and degradation is recommended for long-term investment analyses.

4.5 Optimisation objective and equivalent benefit form
E_b* = arg min(E_b ∈ ℬ) { C_grid(E_b) + AEC(E_b) } (13)
The candidate set ℬ must include 0 kWh and physically marketable storage capacities. ℬ can initially be
{0, 5, 10, 13.5, 15} kWh to match the thesis and later be refined, but results can only be called the best of
the tested capacities unless a sufficiently broad/continuous optimisation has been demonstrated.
NB(E_b) = C_grid(0) − [C_grid(E_b) + AEC(E_b)] (14)
NB(E_b) = Σ[T_i,t E_d,t − T_FIT,t E_c,t] − AEC(E_b) (15)
Equation (15) follows from Eq. (7) when curtailment is zero and there is no grid charging. Therefore E_b*
= arg max NB(E_b). The difference between baseline grid imports and battery-case imports equals
discharge; lost PV exports equal charging. When grid charging or curtailment is allowed, Eq. (14) remains
general, but Eq. (15) needs additional terms. A battery may be economically rejected when 0 kWh yields
the largest net benefit [4], [5].
FINAL RECOMMENDED FUNCTION For the present thesis scope, present Eqs. (1)–(15) as the proposed
hourly simulation-based sizing model, with Eq. (13) the formal cost-minimisation decision and Eq. (15) the
interpretable net-benefit equivalent under specified dispatch assumptions.

5. Tariff dispatch and Australian incentives
5.1 Why a price-aware extension matters
The charge/discharge min rules in Eqs. (3) and (4) intentionally reproduce the thesis priority logic: direct
PV use, excess PV to battery, residual PV to grid, then deficit supplied by battery and grid. This greedy rule
is suitable as a transparent baseline, but is not necessarily optimal under varying import tariffs because

energy might be more valuable at a later evening peak. It should not be described as globally optimal
dispatch merely because the outer loop chooses the cheapest capacity [5], [9], [11].
A TOU-aware extension treats E_c,t, E_d,t, B_t, E_i,t and E_x,t as decision variables and, for each E_b,
minimises Σ[T_i,t E_i,t − T_FIT,t E_x,t] over the horizon, subject to the SOC, power, energy balance and
permitted grid/PV source restrictions. Enforce E_c,t ≤ S_t when PV-only charging is intended, and impose
non-simultaneous charging/discharging where needed. The economic sizing model then compares
optimally dispatched capacities with the same financial objective. TOU results must be explicitly
separated from the original rule-based thesis baseline.

5.2 Modelling the 2026 subsidy without invented amounts
The Australian Government Cheaper Home Batteries Program now provides a discount for eligible smallscale batteries. From 1 May 2026, the incentive is based on eligible usable battery capacity, the
installation-date STC factor and tiered capacity treatment; the posted factor for May–December 2026 is
6.8, with 100% of the factor on the first 14 kWh of usable capacity, 60% for the next 14 kWh and 15%
above 28 to 50 kWh [7], [8]. Eligible batteries have additional nominal-capacity, product, installer and
other requirements. Eligibility cannot be assumed from nominal E_b alone.
u(E_u) = min(E_u,14) + 0.60·min(max(E_u−14,0),14) + 0.15·min(max(E_u−28,0),22) (16)
For a hypothetical eligible installation in May–December 2026, an indicative certificate-weighted
expression is STC_raw = 6.8 × u(E_u), where E_u is the policy-defined eligible usable capacity. Actual
certificates, rounding, pricing, deeming and eligibility must be determined using the current
regulator/REC Registry calculator and installer quote. Do NOT treat STC_raw × an invented dollar value as
a verified rebate [7], [8].
S_gov(E_b) = { verified quote discount(E_u,date,eligibility), if eligible; 0 otherwise } (17)
This formulation allows the current 2026 program to be incorporated without silently equating 30% of the
nominal battery price with the legislated calculation. The simulation figures below use a separate, plainly
hypothetical flat 30% cost reduction solely to illustrate a financial sensitivity, not an Australian policy
quote.

8. Interpretation, screening rules and robustness
8.1 Marginal economic value and feasibility
η_RT = η_c η_d ;

storing 1 kWh PV is useful when η_RT T_import,later > T_FIT,now (18)

This marginal rule screens the operating value of shifting surplus PV to a later deficit in the absence of
capacity/power restrictions; with 0.95 charge and discharge efficiency, η_RT = 0.9025. It is not a sufficient
investment decision rule because installed cost, lifetime, utilisation and changing tariffs must also be
included. A load deficit beyond 3,112 kWh/year avoided imports is impossible under the baseline annual
demand partition and PV-only charging constraints; actual reductions are smaller owing to energy losses
and timing limitations.
